# Ship 30 for 30 essay skill
Edit this file to change how essays are written. No code changes needed.
Adapted from Ship 30 for 30 atomic-essay principles, scaled to a ~1,250 word essay.

## Principles
- One idea per essay. Every section serves a single, specific takeaway.
- Open with a hook: a short, punchy first line that states a strong opinion, a surprising fact or a sharp question. No throat-clearing.
- Short sentences and short paragraphs (1-3 lines). Plain words, like talking to one smart friend.
- Skimmable: descriptive H2 headings, bullets for lists or steps, **bold** only for a few key phrases.
- Be specific: name the guest, the company, the example. Avoid vague advice.
- Ground every claim in the excerpts and attribute ideas to the guest by name. Never invent quotes, numbers or guests. If the excerpts do not say it, leave it out.
- End with one concrete takeaway the reader can apply today.

## Sections
hook | 90 | Start with a '# Title' line (max 10 words), then a 2-4 line hook with a strong opinion or surprising fact. No heading for the hook itself.
context | 200 | Under a '## ' heading, explain the problem and why it matters to a product or growth team.
insight_1 | 250 | Under a '## ' heading, the first key insight. Attribute it to a named guest, add a specific example, then say what it means.
insight_2 | 250 | Under a '## ' heading, a second, DIFFERENT insight. Do not repeat earlier points. Use a short bullet list if it helps.
insight_3 | 250 | Under a '## ' heading, a third, DIFFERENT insight. Include a common mistake people make.
action | 150 | Under a '## ' heading, give 3-5 concrete steps as a bullet list the reader can take this week.
takeaway | 80 | Under a '## The takeaway' heading, finish with one memorable, actionable sentence, then one line inviting reflection.
